If you are sourcing power cords for government contracts, it is imperative that you procure TAA compliant power cords.  But what does TAA actually mean?


The TAA, or Trade Agreements Act, requires that U.S. government agencies buy products that are made or substantially 
transformed in the United States or an approved country.  The [list of countries is longer than you might think](https://www.gsa.gov/sell-to-government/step-1-learn-about-government-contracting/how-to-access-contract-opportunities/help-with-mas-contracts-to-sell-to-government/roadmap-to-get-a-mas-contract/readiness-assessment-for-mas-offerors/look-up-trade-agreements-actdesignated-countries) and includes countries that meet at least one of four criteria required by the U.S. government: 

- Be a signatory of the **World Trade Organization Government Procurement Agreement (WTO GPA)**
- Have a bilateral or multilateral **Free Trade Agreement (FTA) Partnership** with the U.S.
- Have a **Least Developed Country (LDC) Designation** by the United Nations
- Have a **Caribbean Basin Country Designation** which aims to promote growth in that region.

## Is Vietnam a TAA designated country?


As of late 2026, there has been some market confusion about the status of **Vietnam** which **is not a TAA approved country** as it does not meet any of the four criteria. Many companies have shifted from Chinese to Vietnamese manufacturing, and buyers (and some sellers) mistakenly assume "not China" means TAA compliant which is incorrect.

## Country of Origin and TAA

For power cords, the **country of origin of the wire**  is the determining factor for whether a cord is TAA, __not where the ends of the cord are terminated.__ For example, if American or other TAA wire is shipped to China for termination as a power cord, the finished product __is TAA compliant__, but if Chinese, Vietnamese or other non-TAA wire is terminated or finished in the U.S., it is **not TAA**, but rather "American Assembled" and not qualified for government purchase. The COO of the wire dictates TAA compliance, not where the product became a finished good.


## Difference Between TAA and BAA

TAA compliance requires "substantial transformation" of a product in the U.S. or a designated country, meaning that the manufacturing process creates a new and distinct article of commerce. This is not to be confused with the Buy American Act (BAA) that mandates that the majority (65% as of October 2024) of a product must be produced in the United States.
